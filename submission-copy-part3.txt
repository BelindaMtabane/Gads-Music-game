SECTION C — CRITICAL ENGAGEMENT WITH FEEDBACK
(Approximately 560 words)
================================================================================

WHAT DID I EXPECT?

Before the Goethe-Institut session, I expected feedback on whether the chase fantasy works and whether players understood the two-artifact win condition. Because we use AI tools in development, I also expected questions about dialogue — but I assumed criticism would focus on guard difficulty and jump feel, which we had already tuned in earlier internal playtests.

I expected younger players might struggle with controls; I did not expect the youngest tester (age 14) to praise AI-assisted dialogue as the feature that helped him understand the game. That shifted how I value narration relative to pure mechanics.

WHAT SURPRISED ME?

The strongest surprise was unanimous UI criticism combined with positive dialogue clarity. All five players said the UI needs work; four said dialogue and AI narration is simple and straightforward. We had been treating UI as secondary to making the run work, but external players experience menus, fonts, and dialogue boxes first.

Artifact visibility surprised two adult players (P3 and P4). We invested in guard AI and pink-material fixes; players still could not see the goal objects clearly on the lane.

Player P4 gave the densest list: bugs, too short, lower dialogue box, bigger character, speed without shoes, and change "hits left" wording. That matched technical debt we knew about (canvas scale bugs) but had not fully fixed before the event — so public feedback felt harsher than internal testing.

Player P5’s comment that pickup sounds should connect to sneaking sounds was unexpected — an audio design cohesion note we had not considered. Players P2 and P3 both asked for more music, which aligns with our brand but exposes thin audio layering in the current build.

Positive surprise: "Love the idea of music and gaming — great vibe" (player P5) and player P1 liking the sound concept. The core pitch lands; execution and polish lag.

WHAT DID I IGNORE OR CHOOSE NOT TO IMPLEMENT?

Side seats in the environment (player P2): Deferred. Valid set-dressing for a venue fantasy, but requires modelling time with low impact on the core loop before submission.

Full curtain cut-scenes (player P1): Deferred to a future art pass. We lowered and enlarged the dialogue box instead for a faster user-experience gain.

Live AI dialogue during play: Rejected for this milestone — latency, scope, and playtest stability. We keep scripted lines written with AI-assisted drafting offline.

Making the level much longer immediately: Partially accepted. Player P4’s "too short" is noted; we plan more spawn distance but will not rebuild the entire level in the Part 3 deadline window.

Every UI redesign suggestion without prioritization: We implemented font size, panel position, HUD labels, and visibility scales first; full menu redesign is staged, not ignored.

EVALUATION OF FEASIBILITY

Bigger font and lower dialogue box — High feasibility — Implemented
Artifacts and character more visible — High feasibility — Implemented
Rename "Hits Left" on HUD — High feasibility — Implemented as "Lives" and "Artifacts X / 2"
More music — Medium feasibility — Planned in AudioManager
Pickup and sneak sound cohesion — Medium feasibility — Planned sound-effects pass
Base speed without speed shoes — Medium feasibility — Planned tuning of forward speed
Bugs (UI canvas) — High feasibility — Canvas scale fixed
Side seats and curtains — Low feasibility this sprint — Deferred

FINAL JUDGEMENT

Goethe feedback confirmed the idea and challenged the presentation. We integrate: readability, visibility, honest HUD language, and stability. We defer: environment dressing and cinematic curtains. We reject: scope explosions that risk another bug pass.

This matches professional practice: playtests tell you what players feel, not what to build blindly. We documented what shipped versus what is next in our improvement plan and refinements log.

Professional judgement means not agreeing with every comment — it means categorizing feedback, testing feasibility against Unity and time constraints, and shipping targeted improvements that honour the design intent of Rhythm Raiders as a short-session music-and-chase game.
